Optimization Report: 2026-06-22-09-10-03-qr_v2

Scope: the single linalg/qr_v2 optimize-tree run tagged 2026-06-22-09-10-03-qr_v2 on NVIDIA B200 (sm_100), 4 workers, 171 brief-branches. Baseline is torch.geqrf — a serial cuSOLVER loop over the batch (~93k tiny launches, tensor cores idle), geomean 131,465 µs over the 12 benchmark shapes. The shipped best is commit c508b154 at 2,040.9 µs, leaderboard-ACCEPTED (22/22).

Executive Summary

This run found one decisive structural win and then a long stack of refinements grafted onto it: replacing the serial torch.geqrf loop with a custom batch-major blocked compact-WY Householder QR kernel is what broke the problem open (10.8× in a single commit), and every fast result descends from that one lineage. The search was broad in tactics but narrow in strategy — all four workers converged onto the same blocked-kernel basin and spent their budget deepening it (panel fusion → on-chip T → tensor-core trailing → two-level n512 → CUDA-graph host stack), rather than exploring genuinely different algorithms. The two highest-leverage marginal jumps are the blocked kernel itself and the on-chip-W fused trailing update; the end-to-end champion at the top of the stack is a host-dispatch lever (graph capture + zero-copy output pool), not a compute one.

Key takeaways

  • The custom blocked Householder kernel is the lever. Swapping the serial torch.geqrf loop for a Triton batch-major blocked QR jumped to 10.76× in one commit (7f5cdc08); fusing the panel, the T-formation, the trailing update, then adding tensor cores and graph capture carried it the rest of the way to 64×.
  • The biggest genuine marginal is the on-chip-W fused trailing update (0a9b49da, 13.91×) — it keeps W = Vᵀ·A and Y·T in registers so the trailing update never round-trips to HBM. The 23.46× on the router row (dcf3bde4) is larger but is an attribution artifact (it measures the router grafting in the blocked kernel, not dispatch logic).
  • The end-to-end champion is a host-overhead win, not compute. The shipped best c508b154 is fp16x3 precision grafted onto d262aa42 (CUDA-graph capture + rotating output-buffer pool + zero-input-copy); its marginal over the capture stack is ~1.004×. The compute speedup lives entirely in the blocked kernel + fused trailing + tensor-core GEMM beneath it.
  • Tensor-core trailing precision was rediscovered by all four workers — ~135 rows / ~1,900 attempts across every worker independently landed a tf32x3 (and later fp16x3) tensor-core trailing GEMM. Independent rediscovery at that scale is a strong “this is real” signal.
  • The search was a single deep basin, not a diverse frontier. Every alternative algorithm tried — megakernel, two-level nested panel, CholeskyQR2/3, TSQR, tcgen05/TMEM trailing, FP4 — was killed or shelved, and the whole fast lineage stacks on one blocked-QR kernel. Treat the peak step-speedups as ranking signals, not a precise decomposition: they are single-trial measurements taken under multi-worker GPU contention.

Per-shape router / dispatch posts the largest single marginal but the magnitude is an attribution artifact: the 23.46× of dcf3bde4 (“combine custom small-n n≤256 with the in-run batch-major backend for n>256”) is measured against a parent still doing a slow per-shape path, so the jump is really the router grafting in the blocked kernel, not dispatch logic itself. As a standalone mechanism the router is a Python dispatch table keyed on (n, batch) that sends each shape to its best backend. It mattered because the 12 shapes have wildly different optima — n=512/B=640 wants the blocked kernel, tiny n=32 wants a warp-per-matrix path, large-n low-batch wants different tiling/precision — and it is the connective tissue every other technique plugs into (799076e3 rerouted tiny shapes to the backend after proving the custom small-n kernel was slower everywhere). Its value is entirely in what it routes to, not in the routing.

Fused trailing update (W on-chip) is the highest genuine marginal lever (0a9b49da, 13.91×). It rewrites the blocked QR’s trailing-matrix update so each Triton program owns a full-height column strip and keeps W = Vᵀ·A and Y·T resident in registers across two row sweeps — the Y·T product never round-trips to HBM. On the dominant n=512/1024 shapes this is an HBM-bandwidth win (the trailing update is the bulk of the FLOPs and was memory-bound on the intermediate). It stacks directly on the custom blocked kernel and was deepened by all four workers (8772d3b5 replaced the serial per-k V re-read in the wide-T build with a single tensor-core Gram product; 76472eb1 made the wide-T recurrence-free). The crucial leaderboard constraint here was that the fused single-kernel form is board-legal whereas a two-stream variant was rejected — 0a9b49da’s one-kernel-per-strip formulation is what made the fusion shippable.

Custom blocked Householder QR (batch-major) is the foundation the entire fast lineage rests on (7f5cdc08, 10.76× from baseline in one commit, 285 new lines in submission.py). It is a right-looking blocked QR that processes each width-32 column panel across the whole batch in one Triton launch — panel_factor + build_T + a race-free two-kernel BLAS-3 trailing update — collapsing the baseline’s ~93k tiny serial cuSOLVER launches into a handful of batched, tensor-core-eligible kernels. It attacks the baseline’s core pathology (serial, latency-bound, tensor cores idle). This is the run’s defining structural decision; every other technique in the table is a graft onto it.

Fused panel factorization (geqr2) and on-chip T-formation are the two fusions that took the blocked kernel from ~11× to ~16×. fa6bbfb8 collapsed the ~30k tiny gemv launches of the panel factorization into one Triton kernel per matrix with an on-chip column loop (7.59× marginal, B=640 n=512 then 28.7× over baseline); 82903fd3 folded the compact-WY T-matrix formation (the dlarft recurrence) into the panel kernel from resident V, eliminating a ~500-call Python gemv loop and a Vmask reconstruction (3.90× marginal, geomean to 8,157 µs / 16.1×). Both are latency/launch-overhead kills on the panel, which is the serial part of blocked QR. The panel fusion was reached by a single worker lineage (high-impact but lightly corroborated); the T-formation fusion was deepened by all four.

Tensor-core trailing precision (tf32x3 → fp16x3) is the most independently rediscovered technique — ~135 rows / ~1,900 attempts across all four workers (4110e701 splices a tf32x3 tensor-core trailing update; 98e89d90 register-tiles the group-B trailing GEMM; abbeb37a carries the late tf32x3→fp16x3 sweep under graph capture). It targets tensor-core utilization on the trailing GEMM, the kernel’s largest FLOP sink, using 3-pass tf32 (tf32x3) — and later 3-pass fp16 (fp16x3, ~30-bit effective mantissa) — to keep FP32-equivalent accuracy for QR’s orthogonality. Its individual marginal is modest (~2.4×) because it lands on a kernel the panel/trailing fusions already sped up, but its prevalence is the signal: four independent workers converging on the same precision ladder (1-pass fails the diff-guard, 3-pass is the safe floor) means it is robustly real. fp16x3 specifically only pays once the host-dispatch stack amortizes per-kernel launch — that is why it lands last, as the c508b154 graft.

CUDA-graph capture (+ rotating buffer pool, zero-copy) is the end-to-end champion (c508b154/d262aa42, 64.42×) yet has a near-1.0× marginal, which is the whole point: it is a host-side lever, not a compute one. 76204dd5 added a rotating output-buffer pool so the dominant n=512 shape could enter capture without the replay clone that previously made it a loss; d262aa42 then added zero-input-copy replay for the count==1 shapes (n=512+n=1024, ~62% of the geomean weight), giving the “−5.3% host-dispatch stack” relative to the pre-capture kernel; c508b154 grafted the fp16x3 precision change on top. It stacks last, on top of everything above, and was reached only because the kernel underneath it was already fast enough that host dispatch became the residual cost.

Failed Optimization Techniques

Reduced-precision trailing updates without re-orthogonalization were the most-repeated correctness dead-end. One-pass tf32 on the trailing dots (e37e3991 for n=1024, 81df4e28 on all three trailing GEMMs) failed the ranked ill-conditioned shapes (n=512 scaled-residual 24.3 vs allowed ~1; rankdef/nearrank/clustered all below tolerance), and the NVFP4/MXFP4 emulation (198ace1e — explicitly the #1 holder’s submission_nvfp4.py path) failed by ~16% relative-Frobenius GEMM error from e2m1’s single mantissa bit (n=4096 dense raw fp4 scaled-factor 281 vs allowed ~14). Even a 2-MMA tf32 split (000681b5) and the late fp16x2→fp16 (2-MMA→1-MMA) joint reductions (f96bf304, 28b31254) tripped the diff-guard on timed n=512 ill-conditioned shapes. QR’s orthogonality is too precision-sensitive for single- or even double-pass low precision without a re-orthogonalization step; the 3-pass tf32x3/fp16x3 is the accuracy floor the run settled on, and the fp4 attempt skipped the correction step that makes fp4 viable for the actual #1.

Multi-CTA and two-level nested panel factorization failed structurally. The multi-CTA panel (0a9b49da’s sibling qr_panel_mcta, grid (G,B) with a per-matrix barrier) produced wrong reflectors (residual ~800) where it engaged; the two-level NB=32/IB=8 nestings (da184f5e, 737bbcca, 43380c48, 59ef9e50) hit NaN/Inf, broke the wide-T build, or hit a hard Triton wall — tl.dot requires K≥16, and the IB=8 inner Gram/trailing dots have K=8 and cannot use tensor cores at all. The panel carries a serial column dependency that resists CTA-level parallelism without a correct cross-CTA reduction, which none of these landed. (A coarser two-level IB16/NB32 did eventually land in the broader effort, but the aggressive nestings here all failed.)

The geqrt3 recursive-WY panel was a confirmed structural dead end (94bd91fd math-correct but OOM’d shared memory at MAXH≥1024; 7ff6415d OOM’d even at MAXH≤512 once tf32x3 splitting added scratch; the line closed at dce59729). The recursion’s shared-memory footprint forces one block per SM, starving the tensor-core trailing update of occupancy — it makes the panel “cleaner” at the cost of the part that dominates runtime. A textbook case of optimizing the wrong sub-kernel.

Hand-CUDA / tcgen05 trailing substitutions were correct-but-slower. A register-blocked custom-CUDA panel replacing the Triton _panel_factor_kernel (34b4ecd0) regressed and raced (n=512 panel 441 µs vs Triton 264 µs, grid-starved at 0.86 waves/SM); a smem-resident hand-CUDA fused trailing (000681b5) was fully correct but ran FP32 CUDA-core GEMMs ~5× slower than Triton’s tf32 tensor cores; and the gated tcgen05/TMEM trailing probe (32e1c1d1/93a3254b) — although it structurally escaped the register wall — benchmarked 10–14× slower than torch.bmm everywhere because the K-tiled mbarrier round-trip dominates, killing the entire tcgen05 trailing line. The production trailing already runs tl.dot tensor cores at bmm-class throughput, leaving these substitutions nothing to gain.

External-library batched backends (geqrfBatched via load_inline) were a minor dead end: a per-shape geqrfBatched route for n=512 (3b374e81) showed a 1.10× local number that was a cuSOLVER-degradation artifact rather than a real win, and a load_inline shim collided with the auto-generated pybind module (8b1b634e). These add device-pointer-array setup and build/portability risk while offering no win over the custom kernel that had already left the library path entirely.

Unexplored Areas

NVFP4/FP4 tensor cores with re-orthogonalization — the single highest-value gap, because it is how the actual #1 holder wins (NVFP4 + re-orthogonalization, far below 2,040 µs). The run tried NVFP4 exactly once (198ace1e) and it failed the diff-guard precisely because it was used raw, without the correction step. Nobody implemented the gated “fp4-fast-GEMM + cheap re-orthogonalize, fall back on ill-conditioned” combination. The shipped best (2,040.9 µs) is several-fold off #1, and this is the lever that closes it.

BF16 trailing with iterative refinement — only tf32x3 and fp16x3 (both 3-pass, safe) and a failed raw fp4 were tried for the trailing GEMM. BF16 with a single correction sweep — the same re-orthogonalization idea that makes fp4 safe, applied one precision tier up — was never benchmarked, and could roughly halve trailing cost on the dominant n=512/1024 shapes (the bulk of the geomean weight) if guarded on conditioning. It sits in the unexplored gap between the safe 3-pass floor and the failed 1-pass attempts.

The serial panel bottleneck on low-batch large-n (n=2048 B=8, n=4096 B=2) — these shapes run the one-program-per-matrix panel at 2–8 CTA occupancy (88 idle SMs at n=1024 per 76204dd5’s profile). Every aggressive parallelization attempt failed: multi-CTA panel produced wrong reflectors, and the barrier-free TSQR/CAQR panel prototypes (f1c1d1c3 and siblings) were measured grid-starved (0.41 waves, 20.8% SM, 1.08% DRAM) and never landed a net win. A correct TSQR leaf with a log-depth reduction tree to fill the idle SMs is the obvious untried attack on the kernel’s remaining compute floor for these shapes.

CUTLASS / CuTe-DSL kernels — the entire run was Triton + inline NVRTC CUDA. No CUTLASS collective or CuTe-DSL blocked-QR was benchmarked, despite the B200 (sm_100) trailing GEMM being a CUTLASS sweet spot where its collective-builder pipelines typically beat hand-Triton tensor-core code. The trailing update specifically — the largest FLOP sink, where tf32x3/fp16x3 are the current best — is where a CUTLASS path would most plausibly pay off, and it was never tried even once.

A persistent / cross-shape graph capture — capture is currently per-shape-key with kernel relaunch between panels, and the zero-copy win only covers the count==1 shapes (a count==2 extension was measured neutral and disabled, 7bd89b80). A single cooperative-grid persistent kernel spanning the panel sequence (no relaunch), or a capture spanning the batch dimension, was never attempted and would attack the residual launch overhead that per-shape graph capture only partially removes.
