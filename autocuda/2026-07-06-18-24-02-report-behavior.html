Behavior Report: 2026-07-06-18-24-02

Executive Summary

This single optimize-tree run produced 200 scored trials across 18 briefs in 18.37 hours of elapsed time. Mean branch throughput was 3.39 trials/hour (3.81 among the 16 briefs with scored trials); reliability was operationally good despite 155 gaps over ten minutes because the session evidence attributes most long intervals to the single-GPU scheduler, not agent silence.

Cross-run variance is unavailable in single-experiment mode. Within the fleet, nonzero throughput stayed between 2.01 and 5.35 trials/hour around a 3.67 median, and there were no agent-initiated attempts to terminate the experiment early.

Key takeaways

  • Exact concurrency was three active briefs for 17.14 hours, or 93.3% of the run; the longest under-strength stretch was 42 minutes during a worker handoff.
  • The scheduler recorded 28.29 aggregate agent-hours waiting for locks. That was 50.9% of the three worker sessions’ summed walltime; exclusive gpu-0 waits contributed 22.87 hours.
  • brief-1 absorbed the most contention at 8.05 wait-hours, while the worst single lock wait was 460 seconds.
  • No completed brief was more than 2× away from the fleet’s nonzero throughput median. brief-16 and brief-17 had no scored trials because the operator requested graceful termination.
  • The final stop at 12:52 UTC was explicitly operator-directed; all workers reached safe checkpoints and left clean worktrees.

Fleet Behavior

Concurrency Over Time

The requested fleet size was three workers. Across 221 five-minute windows, 202 windows touched three briefs, 10 touched four, eight touched two, and one touched one. The apparent four-brief peak (50 window-minutes) is a bucketing artifact: a completed brief and its successor can both touch one five-minute window. An exact interval sweep never exceeded three simultaneous briefs and measured 17h08m23s at three, 1h04m54s at two, and 8m56s at one.

Five-minute buckets were below the requested strength for 42m13s. The longest exact under-strength stretch was 42m03s, from brief-4 stopping at 03:32:55 UTC until brief-5 started at 04:14:58; it included the five-minute restart of brief-1 and was the only material fleet refill delay.

Brief-Cycle Timing

Using the required manager-row to first non-lifecycle result definition, median handoff latency was 15m15s and p95 was 27m03s. Seventeen briefs exceeded five minutes; brief-9 was worst at 35m30s. These values include implementation, compilation, and validation before the first logged result: manager-row to brief_start was only 42.6 seconds median, 69.2 seconds p95, and 83.7 seconds maximum, so dispatch itself was prompt.

Lock Contention

The five session records contain 668 lock acquisitions and 652 completed releases. Builds completed 198 times and ran for 6m59s, while 454 released gpu-0 operations ran for 17h17m11s. Aggregate waiting was 28h17m32s: 5h25m14s for the shared build gate and 22h52m18s for exclusive gpu-0. Sixteen acquisitions lacked a release record, principally around interrupted or gracefully terminated work, and are excluded from run-duration totals but included in observed wait totals.

The worst wait was 460.048 seconds in brief-1. Total wait by long-lived worker was balanced—9.25, 9.38, and 9.66 hours—but brief-1 alone accumulated 8.05 hours because it spanned three continuations. Aggregate wait was 50.9% of the three workers’ 55.54 combined session-hours, making the one-GPU scheduler the run’s primary throughput constraint.

Per-Brief Reliability

The median nonzero rate was 3.67 trials/hour. brief-6 was fastest at 5.35, while brief-14 was the slowest completed brief at 2.01; neither crossed the 2× outlier threshold. brief-16 reached a validation error and brief-17 reached full validation, but both were stopped before a scored benchmark, so their zero rates are termination artifacts rather than stalled workers.

Other Aberrant Behavior

  • brief-15 contains a second brief_stop row correcting an incorrect long-form SHA suffix in the first stop note. The correction identifies the clean branch tip and does not alter measurements.

Analysis

The fleet was effective at keeping the GPU occupied: released exclusive operations account for 17.29 of 18.37 elapsed hours. Low per-branch trial rates therefore reflect expensive validation, benchmarking, and profiling plus serialized access, not idle agents. The 155 log gaps are the visible consequence of that design: even a productive trial typically spent five to seven minutes executing and often longer queued behind two peers.

Throughput variation is modest after excluding the two operator-stopped briefs. brief-14 is slower because each of its two trials included profiling and a substantial rewrite; brief-6 is faster because it logged more validation outcomes in a shorter search. brief-1 is the main scale outlier in duration and contention, not in rate: it ran three continuations, produced 52 trials, and paid 8.05 aggregate wait-hours.

The run also distinguished fleet dispatch from time-to-first-result. Workers acknowledged new briefs within about a minute, while the first logged trial took a median 15 minutes because logs are written after implementation and validation. Treating that entire interval as manager latency would incorrectly diagnose the orchestrator.

Recommendations: On this one-GPU host, use one benchmark/profile worker plus research or code-preparation workers that do not enter the GPU queue; batch validation and benchmark stages where correctness permits; and add native scheduler telemetry to the behavior CSV so future reports do not need transcript reconstruction. Preserve prompt dispatch and the clean safe-checkpoint shutdown behavior.
