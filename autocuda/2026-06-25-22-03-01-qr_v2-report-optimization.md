# Optimization Report — linalg/qr_v2 (`2026-06-25-22-03-01-qr_v2`)

## Executive Summary

This run found **many small latency wins, no structural one** — it opened on an already-heavily-tuned custom blocked-Householder kernel (the prior run's canonical best), so the entire search lived in the last percent. Three workers across 52 brief-branches spent the bulk of their attempts *disproving* big algorithmic levers (whole-matrix CholeskyQR, MGS2, thread-block-cluster / TSQR device-fill, FP8 GEMMs) and the durable gains came instead from a handful of warp-level micro-optimizations on the dominant per-shape panel kernels. The honest end-to-end improvement over the baseline is **~0.7%** (baseline geomean 1756.07us → clean best `0ace2e05b` ~1744us); the manager's headline `56a93c18` (1738us) sits on the held-out-fragile n2048 demote-skip lineage and is a doc-only commit, so the safe, PR-ready number is the ~1744us figure.

**Key takeaways**

- The single best *honest* lever is the **n512 fused single-reduction pivot look-ahead** — collapses two serial warp-reduction trees into one closed-form pass, peak marginal **1.06×** on its combine graft (`0ace2e05`), independently found by worker-1 (`931546a6`) and banked by the combine.
- The second real win is the **n1024 PAF 4-wide bulk-apply, template-scoped off n512** (peak **1.03×**, `a3e6c9f8`): porting it un-scoped *regressed* n512 +18%, so the win required a compile-time `BULK4` gate by `NWARPS`.
- The **4-wide bulk + shfl_xor butterfly on the small shapes** (n176 mega kernel, n352 `__half` panel) was the most-rediscovered family (~38% of branches, `5dfc0c54`/`4787bb32`/`f7421c0b`) — a ~2–3% per-shape latency cut that survives into the canonical best.
- **Every big algorithmic lever was rigorously measured and killed.** The largest step-speedups in the data (`4a372365` 2.77×, `5372bed6` 1.66×, `8fbcde47` 1.50×, the cluster rows 1.08–1.15×) are *reverts recovering from a regressed experiment*, not new techniques — the attribution caveat the skill warns about. The genuine new-technique ceiling in this run is ~1.06×.
- The search was **diverse in hypotheses but concentrated in outcome**: workers attacked occupancy (cluster/TSQR), precision (FP8/FP4/TF32-Gram), the n2048 8-CTA starvation, and the n4096 serial recon, and *all* of them bottomed out against the same two walls — the benchmark's ill-conditioned members forcing FP64/geqrf, and the device-starved large shapes being occupancy-floored.

**n512 fused single-reduction pivot look-ahead** is the run's best genuine lever. The original `panel_cm_ov_bf16_body` warp-0 look-ahead ran *two* sequential full-`m` reduction trees per column — loop-A computed `ssum = sum(colj*colc)` and reduced, which then gated loop-B's rank-1 update and `next_norm2 = sum(nv^2)` reduce — both on warp-0's per-column critical path. The fused version (`931546a6`, cherry-picked into the combine `0ace2e05`) co-accumulates `s_jc`/`s_cc`/`s_jj` in one `m`-pass with a single 3-wide `shfl_xor` butterfly, then computes `next_norm2 = s_cc - 2·twinv·s_jc + twinv²·s_jj` in closed form (the exact algebraic expansion of `sum((colc - twinv·colj)²)`), with an exact-`sum(nv²)` fallback fired only on catastrophic cancellation (a warp-uniform data property, so no divergence). It touches the OV (outer-V-fold) panel, ~10% of n512-dense; the bottleneck angle is reduction-tree *depth* on the serial pivot path (1 tree/column vs 2). The combine graft measured a 1.06× step over `fcc6682d` and the kept-disjoint integration is bit-identical on the written column — and because worker-1 found it independently of the worker that banked it, it is a real win, not a single-trial fluke.

**The n1024 PAF 4-wide bulk apply** widens the trailing-column update in `qr_panel_apply_fused_kernel` (the dominant n1024 kernel at 41.6% of shape4, found via nsys) from one column per `shfl` butterfly to four, amortizing the reduction over 4 dot-products. The decisive detail is that it had to be a **compile-time `BULK4` template parameter wired by `NWARPS`** (`a3e6c9f8`): turning it on for the shared n512 instances (`<8,*>`) *regressed n512 +18%* because those lean panels are register-bound (`BlockLimitRegs=5`), so the win only exists when scoped ON for the `<32,2,true>` n1024 instance and OFF for n512. ncu pins the residual n1024 floor as inter-CTA, not intra-CTA: barrier-stall imbalance between the pivot stripe and the bulk warps (6.2-cycle "highly different durations per warp"), and Grid=60 on 148 SMs (88 idle). This is why the technique tops out near parity-plus (~1.03× marginal, geomean 1741.7).

**The small-shape 4-wide bulk + shfl_xor butterfly** is the same widening idea applied to the two GPU-bound tiny shapes: the n176 `qr_mega_resident_kernel` bulk (2-wide → 4-wide) and the n352 `cmf __half` panel bulk (1-wide → 4-wide, the n352 bulk was a *full* `warp_reduce_bcast` per column). The `shfl_xor` butterfly lands the warp sum in all lanes directly, dropping the separate broadcast `shfl` (5 `shfl`/var vs 6) on the latency-bound critical path. It bought ~3.0% on n352 (620.5→602us) and ~1.9% on n176 (261.5→256.6us), all guards bit-identical within gate tolerance. It is the **most-rediscovered** family (≈20 branches / ~38%), carried into the canonical best (`5dfc0c54`), which makes it the most-trusted win even though its geomean contribution is small (the small shapes are a minor fraction of the geomean).

**Per-shape coop-panel HELP / launch-knob tuning** is the long tail of sweep work: making the pivot-helper count `HELP` and inner-block `IB` runtime/template knobs on the long-`m` coop panels and sweeping them (`36fe76a1`, `171cdb6b`, `242dedef`). The recurring verdict was that `HELP=2` is the barrier-balanced optimum for both the n1024 OV-coop and n2048 `cm_coop_h` panels (the `HELP` curve is `1 > 2 < 4 < 8`), and that the kernels are *trailing-bulk-warp-bound, not pivot-stripe-bound*, so the lever is flat (~1.005–1.009× at best). This family is mostly negative knowledge — it proves the intra-CTA balance lever is exhausted — but it is high-prevalence because every worker re-swept it.

**The n2048 demote-mask skip** (`_N2048_SKIP_DEMOTE 0→1`, `e7ae379a`/`832401a7`) is a local-rule win: the demote mask exists *solely* for held-out-secret robustness (an embedded ill-cond member in a held-out n2048 MIXED B≥4 batch), so skipping it saves ~60–70us on the scored n2048 shape-5 (~7375→~7300us) with no overlap effect locally. It is a genuine speedup but **held-out-fragile** — the canonical final candidate (`840181d7`) re-enables the mask for safety, which is why the honest headline is ~1744us, not the 1738us the skip-lineage shows.

## Failed Optimization Techniques

**CholeskyQR (whole-matrix, per-panel, and shifted variants).** This was the single most-attempted dead-end and was disproven from every angle. The benchmark's conditioning is the structural wall: the dense n512 members reach cond(A)~1e5, so cond(Gram)~1e10 and `smin²/smax²~1e-10` sit *below any TF32 floor*, forcing an **exact FP64-accumulated Gram + FP64 Cholesky** (`e95bbe3a`, `21f199bd`, `5372bed6` with an Ozaki INT8 exact Gram + custom FP64 DMMA blocked Cholesky). Those FP64/Ozaki ops run ~10–67 TFLOP/s on B200 vs ~960 for TF32, so the full pipeline costs ~6.5× the tuned FP16-Householder on dense shape3 (21.5ms vs 3.3ms) — the brief's "all-tensor-core GEMM" premise fails because the conditioning forbids TF32 in the Gram. The per-matrix *hybrid* (CholeskyQR for well-conditioned members, geqrf for ill-cond, `aca104f8`/`130e89eb`/`2c5386bd`) is correct-on-all-inputs but a ~+59× perf dead-end: the batched cuSOLVER Cholesky/TRSM + O(n³) `orhr_col` recon = ~213ms GPU vs 3.5ms FP16, and the ill-cond fraction still needs serial geqrf, so the "fill 148 SMs with a uniform path" pitch is unachievable. The whole-matrix shifted-CQR3 + Ballard-LU reconstruction (`56a93c18`) was likewise disproven on its own load-bearing test: the Ballard reconstruction is bit-exact and the *dense* n1024 (kappa~1e5) passes the FP64 gate, but the n1024 stress members are kappa~6e301 (3n/4 columns exactly zero) / 2.6e9 / 1.7e12, and shifting all passes to avoid the non-PD Cholesky crash biases the factorization ~1000× past the gate. CholeskyQR only *wins* where geqrf is catastrophic — n4096 — where it already lives in the baseline.

**Block-MGS2 panel.** A full modified-Gram-Schmidt-with-reorthogonalization panel was built, math-gate-verified, and benchmarked (`00ca6dc4`/`d7f71a32`). It validates correct on n1024 dense/mixed/nearrank, but ran ~62× slower (222,900us vs 3588): the FP16 MGS2 R-diagonal collapses for large `n`, the gate over-flags the whole batch to geqrf (184ms), and even sans-geqrf the GPU work exceeds the blocked-Householder. Kill-mode is rank-deficiency, decorrelated from the CholeskyQR family.

**Thread-block-cluster and TSQR device-fill.** Multiple multi-CTA-per-matrix panels were built and verified correct on B200: 2-CTA row-split clusters (`b9fcec36`/`caf1a560`/`568796d6`), column-interleaved clusters (`4a0cac96`), and TSQR-HR cluster panels at K=2/K=4 (`20c49213`/`f2e8a21e`/`0abe58e0`). They achieve the device-fill goal — ncu confirms 0.41→0.81 waves/SM (clusters) and 1.62 waves/SM (TSQR K=4) — but **all regress** (geomean 1846–2488us vs ~1752 baseline) because the per-column `cluster.sync` barriers (~1024 cluster-wide barriers/iteration at n1024) and the TSQR merge-idle (64–71% barrier-stall: only the low partner works per tree level) cost more than the de-starve saves. The cluster-barrier latency is the floor.

**FP8 / FP4 trailing GEMM.** Two FP8-E4M3 single-pass trailing-apply variants were built end-to-end with 3-term GEMM-refinement (`9b90c02d`/`eea56ccb`). Both failed for stacked reasons: the per-block quant overhead exceeds the GEMM saving on the short-K apply; `cublasLtMatmul` returns `CUBLAS_STATUS_NOT_SUPPORTED` (err 13) on the real strided-batch B=640 shape (the heuristic finds an algo at batch=1 but not strided-640); the sm_100 `wmma` FP8 path does not compile; and `cublasLt` needs the banned `stream` construct. Net: FP8 left OFF, a measured dead-end on this apply.

**IB-widening and OB-widening.** Widening the inner block (IB 16→32, `b8171a97`) regressed dense shape3 +18% — the PAF panel smem `m·IB` doubles to 64KB at m=512, dropping occupancy ~7→3 CTAs/SM and exposing the warp-0 pivot `m`-pass; per-column sync is OB-bound, not IB-bound. Widening the outer block (OB=128/IB=64, `4ee0712d`) builds but *fails correctness* — the FP16 trailing apply at w=128 amplifies error past the demote-mask thresholds tuned for w=64 — and OB=64/IB=64 (`74defb83`) crashes with `cudaErrorInvalidValue` on an unsupported launch config. The OB=64/IB=32 two-level geometry is load-bearing for both accuracy and launch-validity. (n352 OB=88, `04aaa7f8`, similarly failed: the `minv_blk4` T-inverse geometry assumes b=64.)

**CUDA-graph capture.** Tried on the n176 megakernel (`9b19d903`) and the n1024/n2048 panel/trailing loop (`d4e7299f`), and explicitly on n32 (`9e651cea`). Measured NEUTRAL: the recurring ~3% inter-kernel gaps brief-15 found are **data-dependency stalls, not launch overhead** — n176 is a single GPU-bound megakernel (0.27 waves/SM, latency-bound on serial Householder), and the n1024/n2048 loops have a trailing data dependency a graph cannot collapse. Graphs only help the truly launch-bound tiny path and that path is already near its floor.

**Another-stream look-ahead pipelining and composition failures.** The brief's headline n2048 lever — pipelining the far outer trailing apply on a *second CUDA stream* to overlap the next panel factor — was built correct (double-buffered events, no buffer race) but **hard-fails the banned-construct scan**: the GPU MODE leaderboard rejects any submission doing work on another stream (`1b819c26`). Among `Combine` attempts, the n2048 device-filling Gram+solve decomposition (`7998c001`) regressed 23.6→31.3ms because the extra kernels' Python-loop launch gaps + atomics exceed the de-starve gain (it needs a C++ fused loop with no Python gaps to pay off), and several n2048 BGS combines hit a geqrf-fallback in the *corner* panel (`c771c424`/`e176b698`) where R-diag shrinks 40→1.9e-8 and the FP32 Cholesky breaks — gram-shift regularization either leaves NaNs or biases the corner factorization past the gate.

## Unexplored Areas

**The device-starved large shapes (n2048 B=8, n4096 B=2) have no occupancy lever left to try.** They dominate the absolute runtime (n2048 shape-5 ~7376us, n4096 shape-6 ~11418us) but every device-fill approach this run tried — clusters, TSQR, persistent work-stealing, device-filling Gram+solve — was measured and regressed against the `cluster.sync`/merge-idle/launch-gap walls. The floor here is intrinsic: at B=8 on 148 SMs the panel kernel is 1-CTA/matrix barrier-wait-bound, and B=2 n4096 is 2 CTAs no matter how many threads are added. The only untried angle is a *single fused CUDA-C loop* (no Python-launch gaps) wrapping the entire n2048 pipeline — workers repeatedly noted "device-fill needs the C++ fused loop to pay off" but none completed one; it is the highest-leverage unexplored item, though the conditioning still pins the ill-cond fraction to serial geqrf.

**The n4096 serial reconstruction (~37% of non-Cholesky n4096 cost) was profiled but never restructured.** The back-substitution `recon_lu_cpp` / `diag_lu` chain is B=2-intrinsic GPU-bound (2/148 SMs, serial dependency chain; `panel_solve` register-capped at 6% occupancy). The one attempt to parallelize it across smem-cooperative warps (`3d140436`) regressed +27% because more threads don't add SMs. A genuinely different recon algorithm (e.g. a blocked/recursive triangular inverse that exposes more parallel work per SM) was never built — only the LU-fold variant (`419c813d`), which failed correctness.

**BF16/TF32 mixed-precision *within the Householder path* (as opposed to replacing it with CholeskyQR) was barely touched.** Every precision experiment targeted the Gram/Cholesky of a CholeskyQR replacement; nothing tried, e.g., a TF32 trailing-GEMM inside the existing blocked-Householder for the well-conditioned dense majority with an FP32 fallback gate, which would keep the proven Householder backbone and only swap the apply precision where the conditioning allows. The n512 `g_prec 0→3` (3xTF32 trailing) attempt (`9fc3177b`) failed the factor gate on the *bad subset*, but a per-member-gated TF32 apply on the *good* subset only was not isolated.

**No external high-performance library path (CUTLASS, cuSOLVER `geqrf`-batched as a tuned fast path, cuDNN) was benchmarked as a candidate baseline for the well-conditioned shapes.** The run is entirely hand-written kernels plus the existing cuSOLVER fallbacks; whether a CUTLASS blocked-QR or a more aggressively-tuned batched library call could beat the custom panel on the dense majority is unmeasured. Given how thin the remaining margin is (~0.7%), a library comparison is more likely to *confirm the kernel is at speed-of-light* than to win, but it would close the loop on the "are we leaving anything on the table" question.
