Analysis

The fleet manager generally replenished slots quickly. The exact-width result is stronger than the five-minute bucket count suggests: 89.1% of elapsed time had all three briefs live, and most short replacement gaps disappear inside a bucket. The notable exception is the 14-minute transition at 10:34 UTC, which accounts for a third of all under-strength time and followed two simultaneous worker completions.

Throughput is governed more by experiment shape than by agent inactivity. Brief 1’s classifier work sustained 18.89 trials/hour, while brief 3’s persistent Jacobi kernels required long compile, validation, and benchmark cycles and reached 9.07. The gap narratives and harness messages consistently show active builds, profiling, or measurement; there is no unexplained silent interval over ten minutes.

The shared B200 is the operational bottleneck. Matched GPU-lock wait alone was 2h29m43s, and build-lock wait added 47m13s. Brief 2 paid the most and also had the longest worker lifetime, four gaps, and two early completion cycles. Its 3.28h fleet-wide lock-wait total is large enough that adding more workers on one GPU would probably reduce per-worker throughput rather than increase measured trials.

Recommendations: keep three workers only when their compile/edit phases are staggered; serialize long NCU captures explicitly and require focused launch filters; and replace the completion/resume loop with a mandatory open-directions check before task_complete. Add first-class Codex custom_tool_call parsing to the report data builder so single-trial briefs and async lock pairs no longer require reconstruction.
