Executive Summary

This single optimize-tree experiment consumed 23.3 aggregate session-hours across 12 workers and two steered sessions, spanning 6.43 elapsed hours, with estimated inference spend of about $941. Inference dominated dollars, especially 1.62B cached-input tokens, while measured walltime also lost 3.28 worker-hours to the single-GPU lock queue; this is one experiment, so there is no cross-run cost comparison.

Key takeaways

  • The covered sessions processed 1.64B tokens; 98.9% were cached input, but those reads still cost about $810 at the public GPT-5.6 Sol rate.
  • Paired records show 2.70h of exclusive GPU execution, 0.14h of builds, and 3.28h of lock wait; the remainder is agent reasoning, editing, orchestration, and trace coverage gaps.
  • Brief 2 was the most expensive worker at about $116 and had two compactions, but no brief exceeded 2x the $78.23 fleet median.
  • The manager and one Claude dashboard session are included as steered rows; reporting subagents launched after shutdown are excluded.

Cost-Optimization Opportunities

  • Reduce inherited context size between trials and continuations. The 99% hit rate is excellent, but 1.62B cache-read tokens still contribute about $810, 86% of estimated spend.
  • Shorten or split the longest worker threads after a stable checkpoint. Brief 2 cost $115.99, used two compactions, and paid the most lock wait; it is 1.48x the fleet median even though it remains below the 2x runaway threshold.
  • Schedule profiles and full benchmarks explicitly around the one B200. Observed exclusive calls outnumber builds by 326 to 145, and matched lock waits (3.28h) exceed matched exclusive execution (2.70h).
  • Extend autocuda report data cost to parse Codex custom_tool_call records and async lock output. The current CSV accurately captures tokens but leaves Codex tool counts blank and under-covers brief 10.

Analysis

The local best improved from 25,511.859 us to 25,150.416 us, about 1.42%, at roughly $664 of estimated inference spend per percentage point. That is a costly marginal gain, but the search did produce independent classifier, spectral-split, and dependent-launch improvements rather than spending the whole budget on one lane.

Dollar cost is overwhelmingly a context-volume problem, not an output-monologue problem. Uncached input contributes about $79 and output about $50, while discounted cache reads contribute about $810. The high cache hit rate prevented a much larger bill, but continuing long threads through multiple pushes retained enormous histories; brief 2 alone read 201.8M cached tokens.

Aggregate session-hours should not be confused with elapsed runtime because three workers and the manager overlap. Within the worker fleet, matched GPU work accounts for 2.70 of 16.57 hours and lock waits for 3.28 hours; the remaining time covers reasoning, source edits, compiler activity outside matched pairs, and incomplete lock telemetry. The observed per-call durations fit the environment’s timeout guidance, so tighter single-call timeouts would not address the main cost shape.

Recommendations: preserve the three-worker search width but start replacement briefs one at a time around profiling; checkpoint and spawn a fresh worker after one compaction instead of repeatedly resuming a large inherited context; and add model-aware, per-session pricing plus native Codex tool parsing to the cost collector. These changes target the two dominant costs: cached context volume and single-GPU queueing.
